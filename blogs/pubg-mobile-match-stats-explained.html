How to Read Your PUBG Mobile Match Stats (And What They Actually Mean)

Your KD is 2.3. Is that good? Your average damage is 340. Is that good? Numbers without context are just numbers — here's how to actually read your own PUBG Mobile stats instead of just staring at them.

K/D Ratio — What It Actually Tells You

K/D ratio gets treated like a scoreboard, but on its own it barely tells you how you actually play. A 3.0 KD from someone who camps the edge of the zone all game and picks off exhausted survivors reads identically to a 3.0 KD from someone who fights through three squads mid-game. Same number, completely different playstyle.

Where K/D actually becomes useful is as a trend, not a snapshot. If your rolling KD across your last 20 matches is climbing, something in your decision-making is improving — better fight selection, better positioning, or both. If it's dropping while your damage per match stays flat, you're probably dying in fights you're already winning on damage, which usually points to positioning or third-party awareness rather than aim.

One quirk worth knowing: PUBG's squad results don't report per-player deaths directly. Most trackers, FragTrack included, use the same widely accepted convention — a #1 placement counts as no death for the match, any other placement counts as one. It's not a perfect substitute for a true individual death count, but it's consistent, and it's what basically every stat site is already using, so your numbers stay comparable.

Average Damage — The Stat That Doesn't Lie

If you only track one number, make it average damage per match. Kills depend on your squad, on who gets the final shot, on luck. Damage doesn't care who finished the kill — it just reflects how much you actually fought. A player averaging 400+ damage a match with a modest KD is contributing heavily even if teammates are cleaning up the kills, and that's a genuinely useful thing to know about your own game, especially if you're the one initiating fights.

Survival Time — Context Matters

Long survival time looks great until you realize it can mean two very different things: playing smart and rotating well, or hiding in a bush avoiding every fight until the game forces you into one. Read survival time alongside damage — high survival with low damage usually means passive play, while high survival with solid damage generally means you're actually contesting fights and surviving them, which is the combination worth aiming for.

Win Rate — Why It's Overrated Alone

Win rate feels like the ultimate stat, but it's heavily dependent on squad quality, especially if you're playing with randoms. A strong individual player stuck with a weak squad can post a rough win rate despite playing every match well. It's worth tracking, just don't treat it as the single verdict on your skill — pair it with your personal damage and placement trends, which are far less dependent on who else is in your squad.

Sample Size — Why One Match Means Nothing

A single incredible or terrible match tells you almost nothing about your actual skill level — it tells you what happened in one 25-minute window with a specific set of opponents, loot, and circle rotations. Judging your form off your last game is like judging a batting average off one at-bat. Look at rolling averages across at least 15-20 matches before drawing any real conclusion about whether you're improving, plateauing, or slipping.

Actually Tracking It Over Time

The honest reason most players never learn from their stats isn't a lack of interest — it's that manually logging kills and damage after every match gets tedious fast, and most people quit doing it within a week.
