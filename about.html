About

Built by players who were tired of guessing.

FragTrack started as a weekend script for reading our own squad's match screenshots. It's still early — a small but growing group of players use it today — but the reason for building it hasn't changed.

Where this came from

A few seasons back, a handful of us were grinding ranked PUBG Mobile together most evenings, and every post-match conversation went the same way: someone would squint at a screenshot on their phone, argue about whether the last fight was actually a good trade, and nobody could say for certain because none of us were tracking anything beyond our in-game rank. We'd win a match and still not know if our headshot rate had actually improved, or if we were just landing lucky shots.

So one of us wrote a small script to pull numbers off end-of-match screenshots automatically instead of typing them into a spreadsheet by hand. It was rough — it broke on half the phone resolutions we tried it on — but it worked often enough that we kept using it every night. Other squads we scrimmed against asked to try it. That script is, more or less, the thing that grew into FragTrack.

What we're actually building

FragTrack reads a PUBG Mobile match screenshot and turns it into numbers you can actually use: damage per match, weapon accuracy, survival time, rank point movement, and how all of that trends over your last twenty games instead of just one. Nothing here requires linking your game account or installing anything on your phone — you upload a screenshot, the model reads it, and you get a dashboard.

We're not trying to be a full esports management suite or a social network for gamers. The scope is intentionally narrow: read the screenshot correctly, show the trend honestly, and stay out of your way otherwise.

How we think about the product

Accuracy over polish

An analytics tool that reads your stats wrong is worse than no tool at all. When our OCR isn't confident about a number, we flag it instead of guessing — even if that makes the dashboard look less finished.

Your screenshots stay yours

Match screenshots are encrypted and only ever used to generate your own stats, unless you explicitly opt in to help improve the model. We don't sell player data — there's more detail on this on our privacy page.

We read what you send us

Most of the changes we've shipped came directly from players messaging us about a broken chart or a missing weapon in the tier list. We're still small enough that this works.

Where we are today

FragTrack is still in its early days. Right now it's mostly solo ranked players and a couple of small squads using it to review their own matches — including the coach dashboard for scrim reviews. The team behind it is small too — this isn't run by a large studio, and we're deliberately keeping the product focused rather than chasing every feature request that comes in before the core experience is solid.

If you want to see what the dashboard actually looks like, the product section on our homepage walks through it, or you can just start tracking for free and upload your last match.

Questions or feedback?

We'd genuinely like to hear from you, whether it's a bug report, a feature request, or just feedback on a chart that didn't make sense. Reach us through the contact page.
