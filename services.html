Services

Everything included in your dashboard.

A full breakdown of what you actually get when you sign up — no upsell tricks, no hidden tiers. Here's exactly what's inside FragTrack.

1. Dashboard Overview

The moment you sign in, you land on a summary of your team's performance: matches played, total kills, win rate, and overall K/D ratio, plus a Performance Overview chart you can filter by stat (kills, and others) and time range (7 days, and beyond). It's meant to answer "how are we doing right now" in one glance, without digging through individual matches.

2. Match History

Every match you've logged or uploaded shows up in the Matches tab — map, placement, placement points, total kills, total points, and MVP for that match. You can filter by mode (All Matches, Tournament, Scrims, Daily Battle) to isolate exactly the games you want to review.

From here you can also download a full report as a PDF — a snapshot of your matches and stats you can save, print, or send to a coach or teammate without them needing to log into FragTrack themselves.

3. Performance Analysis

The Performance section breaks your stats down four ways: Overview, K/D Analysis, Rank Analysis, and Win Analysis. You get your K/D ratio trend over time alongside your top stats — best placement, most kills in a single match, total points, average points per match, and total wins.

One thing we're upfront about: PUBG squad results don't report who died and who didn't per player, so we use the same convention most stat trackers use — a #1 placement counts as no death, and any other placement counts as one death. It's not perfect, but it's consistent and it's how the wider community already reads K/D from squad results.

4. Stats Analyzer — Screenshot AI

Upload a PUBG squad match result screenshot and our AI reads the map, placement, and every squad member's name and kills automatically — no manual typing. Right now, this reads Erangel, Miramar, and Rondo match-result screenshots specifically; other maps aren't supported yet, and we'd rather tell you that upfront than have a screenshot silently fail on you.

5. Stats Analyzer — Session Logging

Not every match happens through a screenshot moment — tournaments and scrims often run as a paid session with an entry fee per match. The Log Session tool lets you set the mode (Tournament, Scrims, etc.), the date, how many matches you're playing, and the entry fee per match, and it calculates your total investment for that session as you go. Once set, it walks you through logging each match's result in the session.

6. Session History

Every session you've logged — tournament or scrim — lives here with its mode, date, number of matches, entry fee, total investment, prize won, final rank, and whether it ended in profit or loss. You can search by mode, filter by date range, and open any session to view or edit it.

7. Investment Dashboard

If you're playing paid tournaments regularly, this is where it actually matters: total sessions played, total money invested in entry fees, total prize money won, and your net earnings across everything — plus a profit-vs-loss breakdown and a chart of how your session ranks are distributed. It's built to answer one blunt question honestly: are paid tournaments actually making you money, or costing you money.

8. Players

A simple roster view — total players, combined matches played, kills, and points across your roster, plus each player's assigned role (IGL, Fragger, etc.). Add or remove players from here as your lineup changes.

9. Leaderboards

Rankings across five views: Players, Teams, IGL (In-Game Leaders), Fraggers, and Esports Teams — each showing matches, kills, and points so you can see where you or your roster stack up. Leaderboards are visible to other FragTrack users by design; see our Privacy Policy for details.

10. Analytics

A deeper look at trends: matches played, win rate, K/D ratio, a kills-trend chart over your recent matches, and a breakdown of how your matches split across modes (Tournament, Scrims, Daily Battle). A separate Teamplay view looks at the same numbers from a squad-performance angle rather than just an individual one.

11. Team Management

Your team page shows your team's profile (name, region, squad type, when it was created), combined team stats, and a shareable join code — anyone who enters it joins your roster and their stats become visible to the team. You'll also see a breakdown of roles across your roster (how many IGLs, how many Fraggers) and can manage the full player list from here.

12. Notifications & Support

General notifications and a built-in support ticket system live in the same place. If something's not working or you have a question, open a new query and a real person answers it — you can see the full history of your past tickets and their status (answered, pending, etc.) right there.

What's not included yet

To be upfront: the AI screenshot reader currently supports Erangel, Miramar, and Rondo match-result screenshots only — other maps aren't read automatically yet, though you can still log those matches manually through Session Logging. If something doesn't work the way you'd expect, tell us through the contact page or open a Support ticket from inside the dashboard.

Getting started

There's no complicated setup. Create an account, add your players, and either upload a screenshot or log your first session — your dashboard builds itself from there. If you want to see the interface before signing up, the product preview on our homepage walks through part of what it looks like.
